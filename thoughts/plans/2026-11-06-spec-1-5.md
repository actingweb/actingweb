## Overview

The sweep found 144 places where `docs/protocol/actingweb-spec.rst` (1.4) and
the library disagree. This plan settles every one: the specification is
rewritten where the library's behaviour is the working design, and the library
is fixed where its behaviour is a defect. The result is a version 1.5 the
library conforms to, and announces. It also makes outbound callbacks to
several subscribers concurrent and removes `FanOutManager`, which was never
connected to the publish path. Lands in 3.16 (minor, tag held), after the six
plans that merged before it and before `3-16-release-validation`.

About a third of the sweep's library fixes landed with those six plans. What
is left here is five groups: `/meta` and the list-item paths (Phase 1); the
unhandled-callback and `/www` answers, the webhook flag, two trust answers
and the passphrase (Phase 2); deletes and a few diff details on the
publisher, and three resync faults on the receiver (Phase 3); the sender
(Phase 4); the text (Phase 5).

Row identifiers (A1, P5, S8b, …) are the sweep's.

## Decisions Made

- **Full conformance.** Every row is settled on one side. The library is the
  working code and the specification a theoretical design, so the
  specification follows the library unless a deviation is a defect.
- **The specification is generic.** It describes the protocol, not the Python
  library. Nothing about the library's state, its extensions or what it does
  not implement goes into it. Library-specific surface (the `/items`
  endpoint, `?index=N`, the error bodies, the OAuth and MCP endpoints, the
  extra trust fields) stays in the library's documentation; where a peer
  could meet it, the specification says implementations MAY offer more. The
  two "The ActingWeb library" notes the security plans added
  (`actingweb-spec.rst:1163`, `:1221`) are rewritten as rules or removed.
- **Each deviation was classified** as a defect, a necessity or a matter of
  taste (sweep, "Proposed dispositions"). Defects are fixed in the library;
  the other two are specification edits.
- **Owner checks.** The library changes for: a non-JSON value labelled
  `text/plain` (P7); path-addressed list items as the specification describes
  them (P1 to P4). The specification changes for trustee access, which needs
  no trust relationship (T8). The extra `/meta` paths are defined in the
  specification (A8). [Updated 2026-11-15] Default deny (T22) landed with
  `grant-enforcement-per-route` as "a category the grant mentions but does
  not match is denied; one it does not mention follows the access table".
- **Small library changes on silent rows:** an unknown granularity answers
  400 (S19); `PUT /properties` answers 405 (P19). [Updated 2026-11-15] A
  repeated trust request (T6) is the security plan's re-pair, landed.
- **Outbound delivery** [Updated 2026-11-15, owner]: callbacks for one write
  are sent concurrently, at most `max_concurrent_callbacks` at a time for
  that write (default 4), and subscribers on the publisher's own host one at
  a time. Off the event loop, and with `with_sync_callbacks`, the write
  returns when every callback has answered or timed out; on a running loop
  without it the callbacks are tasks the library keeps a reference to and
  does not await, as today. `FanOutManager` and the three settings nothing
  reads are removed. The larger design (back-off, a per-peer breaker, a
  payload cap) becomes a todo.
- **A delivery budget** [2026-11-15, owner]: a setting gives one write a
  total time for delivering its callbacks. Off by default. When it runs
  out, sends not started are skipped and sends in flight end with it; the
  diffs are stored either way.
- **The own-host rule has a switch** [2026-11-15, owner]: serial is the
  default; a deployment without a shared handler pool (one request per
  function instance) turns it off and its own-host subscribers are sent
  under the same bound as the others.
- **Anonymous webhooks** [2026-11-15, owner]: a flag on the callback hook
  registration (`allow_anonymous=True`). POST only, application-named
  callbacks only, never the subscription or permission paths and never the
  wildcard. The hook verifies its caller itself, so it is given the request
  headers and the raw body.
- **The callback timestamp** [2026-11-15, owner]: callbacks send the ISO
  form with `Z` that the polls send; every recording that carries the old
  form is re-recorded in Phase 3, with a check that nothing else in it
  changed.
- **The gap resync** [owner, 2026-11-07]: it runs the application's data
  hook for what it applies and keeps the callback that triggered it.
- **Deletes** [2026-11-15]: one representation, the JSON-encoded `""`, from
  every writer. The level that was deleted is sent `""`; a broader level
  `{name: ""}`. A deleted list stays `delete_all`.
- **Clearing all properties** [2026-11-15, owner]: one diff, one sequence
  number and one callback per subscriber, carrying every deleted name.
- **A fetched diff is consumed** [2026-11-15, owner]: the specification is
  written to it (S11), and says how a subscriber whose fetch was lost
  recovers. No library change.
- **The overlap rule** [Updated 2026-11-15]: as landed. Each subscription is
  delivered independently; a subscriber that holds a whole-target
  subscription and a narrower one from the same peer stores from the
  whole-target one, whatever its granularity. The specification says a
  subscriber MUST apply a change once and leaves the choice to it.
- **The passphrase is not returned to a peer, and an admin peer cannot arm
  trustee access** [2026-11-15, owner]: `GET /<actor>` returns the
  passphrase to the creator and the trustee only, and only the creator and
  the trustee may change the trustee root or the creator. An admin peer
  could otherwise keep access after its trust was deleted. No automatic
  rotation: the changelog tells an operator that an admin peer of an earlier
  version may hold the passphrase, and how to change it.
- **The announced version** moves to `1.5` in the last phase, when the
  library conforms.
- **All specification text lives here**, including the list-diff additions
  the list port left unwritten (an empty `extend`, `clear` then `extend`,
  the `spanned` and `bytes` reference form, negative indices, `delete_all`
  defined).

Deduced in the second draft; each confirmed by the owner on 2026-11-15
("Ok to all"):

- **Optional mechanisms stay optional, in generic words.** Back-pressure, the
  circuit breaker, payload limits and compression are written so an
  implementation without them conforms: a sender that does not act on 429 or
  503 treats them as a failed delivery, and the stored diff with polling is
  the recovery every implementation has. This turns two requirements of 1.4
  into options ("the actor MUST" downgrade at `actingweb-spec.rst:3228`,
  "Receivers MUST handle partial operations" at `:3239`); the 1.5 changelog
  block lists them as changes, not clarifications.
- **`/meta/version` is the application's version**: a builder setting, with
  the library's version as the default when unset.
- **`subscriptionbatch` stops being advertised.** The owner's answer was that
  nobody needs it and it is not to be introduced; nothing in the library or
  the two consumers acts on it, and "advertise what is served" (A11) is this
  plan's own rule. The three `PeerCapabilities` helpers that read tags no
  specification defines (`supports_batch_subscriptions`,
  `supports_health_endpoint`, `supports_stats_endpoint`) stay: they read
  what a peer says, and removing one of three would be arbitrary. The
  alternative is to keep advertising an undefined tag.
- **An unregistered trust type answers 404 on both permission endpoints**
  (T19); one of them answers 500 today.
- **A clear registers its one diff from a snapshot of the names, before it
  deletes.** Registered after, a fault at that one registration would lose
  the notification for every name, and a retry would find nothing to
  delete.
- **A hook that ran and returned a falsy value keeps today's answer.** Only
  "no hook is registered for this name" becomes 403 or 404.
- **`max_concurrent_callbacks` is the bound's name.** The setting exists on
  `with_subscription_processing` and is read by nothing; making it live
  avoids removing one name and adding another for the same thing.
- **The path-addressed item forms run the hooks of their `?index` twins**
  (`get` with `[str(n)]`, `put` with `[name, str(n)]`); DELETE and the POST
  append, which have no twin, run `delete` and `put` with `[name, str(n)]`.
  The three existing forms already disagree with each other; this plan does
  not unify them.
- **An anonymous webhook body is capped at 1 MB** (413 above it); nothing
  caps it today, and an authenticated caller is not affected.
- **A callback with data and no `sequence` answers 400**, as a
  low-granularity one already does; today it is taken as a duplicate and
  dropped with 204.
- **A `none` subscription's resync is the marker it polls**, not nothing:
  the publisher stores the `type: resync` diff it already stores for a peer
  without a callback endpoint, and the library's own poll acts on it.
  Without it a `none` subscriber would never learn of changes made while
  suspended.
- **The demo's webhooks:** `email_verify` is a link a browser opens with
  GET and stays unflagged; `sms_webhook` and `payment_webhook` are flagged
  and refuse a request whose shared-secret header does not match a
  configured value (refusing all when none is configured).

## What We're NOT Doing

- **Describing the Python library in the specification** - decided above.
- **Back-off, a circuit breaker, a payload cap with downgrade, compression** -
  a design of its own, filed as
  `thoughts/todo/outbound-callback-backpressure.md`; the specification keeps
  them as optional mechanisms.
- **Defining or building `subscriptionbatch`** - no caller on either side
  (owner).
- **Changing the overlap rule or the creation-time access check** - both
  landed and are recorded (`WD15`, `CHANGELOG.rst` `Unreleased`); only their
  specification text is written here.
- **Making a diff fetch repeatable** - the specification follows the library
  (owner); a non-consuming fetch would need the receiver to acknowledge every
  diff or queues grow.
- **Unifying the hooks and out-of-range answers of `?index`, `/items` and the
  path forms** - a wire change to forms consumers use, with no defect behind
  it.
- **Blocking a running event loop until callbacks answer** - a synchronous
  function cannot await, and a subscriber served by the same loop could then
  never answer.
- **MCP transport gaps** (O8: batches, SSE, `listChanged`, resource
  templates) - conformance to MCP revisions, not to this specification, and
  already in `thoughts/todo/mcp-batch-receive.md` and
  `mcp-oauth-connector-conformance-followups.md`.
- **`/sessions`, `onewaytrust`, `proxy`** - optional in the specification and
  not offered; `thoughts/todo/sessions-endpoint-and-advertised-options.md`
  keeps the first.
- **All-or-nothing `POST /properties`** (P8) - per-key acceptance with hooks
  is the working design; the specification is rewritten to it.
- **The rest of subscriber fault hardening** (keying applied diffs, the claim
  races) - `thoughts/todo/subscription-subscriber-fault-hardening.md`, minus
  the two items Phase 3 takes (the failed baseline, and a property named
  `error` read as a failed fetch).
- **The full-state fallback for a peer without resync** -
  `thoughts/todo/full-state-fallback-replica.md`; reached only for an older
  peer.
- **Removing the unrouted `CallbackOauthHandler`** - exported and documented,
  so its removal is a public-surface decision; Phase 5 files it as a todo
  and the specification stops describing a per-actor OAuth binding (O5).
- **Rotating the passphrase automatically, or a REST route to rotate it** -
  the owner chose to hide it and close the trustee route; a passphrase an
  admin peer already read is an operator step in the changelog.
- **A process-wide bound on callback sends or tasks** - the bound is per
  write (owner); the process-wide one is the back-pressure todo's.
- **The sequential-PostgreSQL concurrency test**
  (`thoughts/todo/callback-concurrency-test-sequential-postgresql.md`) - the
  receiver's claim, which Phase 4 does not touch.

## Phase 4: concurrent delivery, and `FanOutManager` removed

### Changes

- `actingweb/actor.py`
  - `register_diffs`: the loop prepares each subscriber's diff (sequence,
    store, filter, body) as today and collects the sends; one sender
    delivers them after the loop. `_send_resync_callbacks` uses the same
    sender.
  - `_send_callback_sync` and `_send_callback_async` are lifted out of
    `callback_subscription` so the sender can call them.
    `callback_subscription` called directly still sends, as its callers in
    four test files expect; `register_diffs` asks it for the prepared send
    instead.
  - The sender. A subscriber is on the publisher's own host when the
    origin of its trust base URI (scheme, lower-cased host, port with the
    scheme's default filled in) equals the origin of the publisher's root.
    Own-host subscribers are sent one at a time: each needs a handler
    thread of the same process to answer, and a low-granularity one a
    second for its fetch. The others are sent at most
    `config.max_concurrent_callbacks` at a time (default 4), counted per
    write. The two groups run alongside each other, so a write waits for
    the longer of them, not the sum. A subscriber that is owed two ordered
    callbacks (the clear and the full state of the fallback for a peer
    without resync, `actor.py:5356-5366`) is one unit: its second is sent
    after its first.
    - Off the event loop, or with `sync_subscription_callbacks`: a thread
      pool created for the call when there is more than one remote
      subscriber, each send run in a copy of the caller's context
      (`contextvars.copy_context`), the own-host sends on the calling
      thread, and the call returns when every send has answered or timed
      out (the existing 5 s and 10 s; 30 s for a resync).
    - On a running loop without the flag: tasks, the remote ones under an
      `asyncio.Semaphore` of the bound and the own-host ones under a
      semaphore of one, each held in a module-level set until done, not
      awaited. Nothing is sent from the loop thread itself. The write
      returns before they answer, as `WD5` records today.
  - The budget. With `callback_delivery_budget_seconds` set, the sender
    notes when delivery starts; each send's connect and read timeouts are
    clipped to what is left, and a send that would start with nothing left
    is not made. So no send outlives the budget and the write returns
    within it plus the time to prepare the diffs. Every diff was stored
    and numbered before any send, so a subscriber that was skipped sees a
    gap at its next callback and polls, or polls on its own schedule; a
    skipped resync is recorded as missed. The budget applies wherever the
    write waits (off the loop, and with `sync_subscription_callbacks`);
    tasks on a running loop are not awaited and keep their own timeouts.
  - `serialize_own_host_callbacks` (default true). When false, own-host
    subscribers join the others under the bound.
  - Each send returns its result. Off the loop, a resync that was not
    delivered (a refused connection, a timeout, a 503 from a subscriber
    that could not fetch its baseline) is recorded as missed with or
    without `with_sync_callbacks`, so the next resume sends it again
    (`actor.py:5379` returns "scheduled" and `:5089` discards the result
    today).
  - Callback sends no longer write `Actor.last_response_code`. The
    attribute stays for the trust and peer calls that read it.
- `actingweb/subscription_config.py`, `interface/app.py`
  (`with_subscription_processing`) - two new keywords,
  `callback_delivery_budget_seconds` (default `None`, no budget) and
  `serialize_own_host_callbacks` (default `True`).
  `max_concurrent_callbacks` is read and defaults to 4 (10 today, read by
  nothing).
  `max_payload_for_high_granularity`, `circuit_breaker_threshold` and
  `circuit_breaker_cooldown` stop doing anything but stay in the signature
  as keywords whose only effect, when passed, is a `RuntimeError` that
  names the setting and `docs/migration/v3.16.rst`, as
  `with_legacy_property_index` does.
- Removed: the contents of `actingweb/fanout.py`, which becomes a stub
  that raises the same pointer on import (the documented import is `from
  actingweb.fanout import FanOutManager`); its exports
  (`actingweb/interface/__init__.py:11`, `:92-93`), with a module

### Deployment shapes

What the sender does in each, with the defaults and with the settings a
deployment of that shape would choose. A send takes at most 15 s (5 s to
connect, 10 s to read); a resync 30 s.

- **A long-running server (FastAPI or Flask).** Handler threads are shared
  by every request, and a subscriber on the same process needs one to
  answer. Defaults: remote subscribers four at a time, own-host one at a
  time, no budget. The risk is the handler pool, covered by the sizing
  rule and the two-write test.
- **One request per function instance behind a gateway that gives up at
  30 s** (the consumer's shape: Mangum on Lambda, `with_sync_callbacks`
  on, `../actingweb_mcp/application.py:581-583`; its function timeout is
  120 s in `serverless.yml`, so the function outlives the caller's wait;
  every subscriber is on its own origin, `add_actor_type("myself", …)` at
  `:567-571`). Nothing is shared between requests: a callback to the same
  deployment is served by another instance. With the defaults this shape
  is sent exactly as today, serially, and two subscribers that hang
  already pass 30 s (the caller gets a gateway error, the function keeps
  sending, and a client that retries the write produces a second diff).
  With `serialize_own_host_callbacks=False` its subscribers are sent four
  at a time, so four that hang cost 15 s where they cost 60 s; the price
  is function instances running at once, up to the bound per write plus
  one each for a low-granularity fetch back. With
  `callback_delivery_budget_seconds=20` the write answers inside the
  gateway's limit whatever the subscribers do.
- **An event loop without `with_sync_callbacks`.** The write does not wait;
  tasks are kept until done. On a function platform the instance may
  freeze before they finish, which is why that shape sets the flag.

The library sets neither new setting by itself and does not detect the
platform; the deployment guide describes the two shapes.

### Failure Scenarios

- A pool per write under load - created only for a write with more than one
  remote subscriber, at most the bound's threads, gone when the write
  returns; a process-wide cap is the todo's.
- One actor whose subscribers hold connections open - it delays its own
  writes by at most the timeouts per batch of the bound (15 s per batch;
  20 subscribers, 75 s where the serial sender took 300 s). The sender
  shares nothing between writes, but on FastAPI every write shares the
  handler pool: a write holds one thread and its subscribers' requests
  back to the publisher up to the bound more, so two such writes can fill
  the default ten. Serial sending held two. The sizing rule is documented
  and the two-write test is the check.
- Many subscribers on the publisher's own host - serial as today, so no
  better and no worse than 3.15.
- A budget that ends while a send is in flight - the send's own timeout was
  clipped to the budget, so it ends too and is counted as not delivered;
  its diff is stored, and the subscriber recovers by the gap at its next
  callback or by its poll. A subscriber at high granularity that receives
  nothing further stays behind until it polls: unchanged from a send that
  times out today.
- A budget set lower than a healthy subscriber needs - deliveries are cut
  that would have succeeded; the guide says to set it under the gateway's
  limit, not as a tuning knob, and it is off unless set.
- The own-host switch turned off on a long-running server - the handler
  pool can fill as the first review found; the setting's documentation
  says which shape it is for, and the default is serial.
- More function instances at once per write on the function shape - up to
  the bound plus the fetches back; an account's concurrency limit is the
  ceiling, and the bound is the setting that lowers it.
- A peer that registers an alias of the publisher's host as its base URI -
  it is treated as remote and its callbacks are concurrent, which costs
  the handler threads above and nothing else; an operator who serves one
  deployment under several origins is told so in the deployment guide.
- Tasks on a running loop accumulate when writes outpace slow subscribers -
  bounded per write only, each task ending at its timeout; the
  process-wide bound is the todo's.
- A subscriber now receives callbacks from one publisher concurrently with
  another's - unchanged; each subscription's own sequence orders its diffs.
- Two writes to one actor in quick succession deliver to one subscriber out
  of order - possible before (two requests) and now; the receiver's sequence
  tracking handles it.
- `with_sync_callbacks` on a running loop - blocks the loop while the pool
  sends, as the serial sender does today; the documentation says the
  setting is for handlers that run off the loop.
- An application passed a removed setting - a `RuntimeError` at
  configuration with the guide's section; a `REMOVED` entry names them.

### Review of the second draft, 2026-11-15

Five evaluators read the draft against the tree, then the outside voice
(codex, run outside the sandbox, and a fresh-context reviewer) was given
the plan and their findings. Everything below is folded into the phases
above; this is the record of what the review changed. Two findings went to
the owner (the passphrase, the timestamp); the rest were mechanical or are
listed under "Decisions Made" as deduced, and confirmed since.

#### Architecture

- **Critical: the timestamp change (S7) touches about 124 recordings, not
  two** (`actor.py:4025`; `tests/integration/_wire.py:788-789` keeps the
  format class). Phase 3 now takes every such row and proves the others
  changed in that token only.
- **High: the handlers cannot tell "no hook" from "a hook returned a falsy
  value"** (`interface/hooks.py:788`, `:816`). The registry's result
  changes, not a handler helper.
- **High: the flag had nowhere to live and "POST only" was not decidable**
  (`hooks.py:363`, `:798`; `handlers/callbacks.py:93-94`, `:152-153`,
  `:183`, `:669`). Phase 2 states the storage, the wildcard refusal, the
  real verb and the skipped authorise.
- **High: a sweep inside `apply_resync_data` would run from ten callers**,
  `rebuild_subscription` among them. It is one extracted function with

- Medium: seven more callers of the baseline fetch test for `None`; the

  "409 with no hook before it" would add a read to every write (dropped:
  the hook runs, nothing is written); the two send functions are closures.

#### Security

- **High, a former admin peer: hiding the passphrase did not close the

#### Scalability

  because each subscriber calls back into the publisher

The owner asked that the consumer's deployment be designed for. Read from
`../actingweb_mcp` (`serverless.yml`, `application.py`), not run: the
